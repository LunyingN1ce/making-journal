# Week 09

## Independent Study

This week I continued developing my emotional data visualisation project and focused on improving the functionality of the prototype.

The previous version mainly visualised emotional patterns across four weeks using colour and a grid layout. While it helped users identify emotional trends, it did not allow them to actively record emotional experiences.

This week, I expanded the project into a more interactive emotional tracking system. The goal was to move beyond displaying emotional data and begin exploring how users could record emotions, reflect on experiences, and observe emotional patterns over time.

---

## Project Development

The main development this week was creating an emotion input system in p5.js.

The updated prototype now includes:

- Emotion selection
- Date selection
- Time period selection
- Emotion intensity slider
- Trigger selection
- Colour selection
- Reflection notes
- Mood filter
- Time filter
- Weekly emotional overview

This version allows users to actively contribute emotional records instead of only viewing existing data.

The emotional categories include:

- Happy
- Calm
- Stress
- Sad
- Tired

Each day is divided into:

- Morning
- Afternoon
- Night

The visualisation continues to display emotional patterns across four weeks using a grid structure.

---

## Design Decisions

One important design decision was adding emotional triggers.

Earlier versions only recorded emotions. However, I realised that emotions are often connected to specific activities and situations.

To support this idea, I added trigger categories including:

- Assignment deadline
- Study
- Work
- Family
- Friends
- Exercise
- Gaming

This allows users to record not only what they feel, but also what may have influenced that emotion.

I also added an intensity slider. This helps users describe how strongly they feel a particular emotion rather than treating all emotions equally.

Another addition was the reflection note section. Users can write short comments about their emotional experiences and create a more personal emotional record.

---

## Interaction and User Experience

This week I focused on making the project more interactive.

The Mood Filter allows users to focus on specific emotional categories.

The Time Filter allows users to compare emotional patterns across different times of the day.

I wanted the interface to remain simple and approachable, so I continued using circles, colours, and grid layouts instead of more complex charts.

The goal was to make emotional information easy to understand while still allowing users to explore their emotional records in different ways.

---

## What I Learned

This week helped me understand that emotional data becomes more meaningful when users can record context alongside emotions.

Recording emotional triggers, intensity levels, and reflection notes provides more information than simply recording emotional categories.

I also learned that interaction plays an important role in emotional tracking. Allowing users to add their own records creates a stronger connection between the user and the data.

At this stage, I was mainly focused on helping users become more aware of emotional patterns across time. I believed that visualising emotional records could encourage self-reflection and help users better understand their emotional experiences.

---

## Reflection

The project is gradually moving from a simple emotional visualisation towards a more complete emotional tracking system.

By adding emotional input features, the prototype now supports both emotional recording and emotional observation.

Although the project can now show emotional patterns across different weeks and time periods, I am still exploring how emotional data can become more useful for users.

For now, the focus remains on emotional awareness and emotional reflection. Helping users recognise emotional patterns is an important first step before exploring how emotional data might support emotional wellbeing in future development.
