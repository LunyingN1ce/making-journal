## Independent Study

This week I continued developing my emotional data visualisation project. I focused on research, visual references, and planning the structure of the project.

My original idea was to use colours and visual organisation to help people better understand emotional experiences. During this week, I also started thinking about how emotions could be organised and recorded over time. Rather than focusing only on emotional imagery, I became interested in how emotional patterns could be visualised through colour, time, and interaction.

At this stage, I am exploring how emotions can be translated into visual language. Instead of using traditional graphs or numerical charts, I want to communicate emotional states through colour relationships, visual structures, and time-based organisation.

---

## Visual Research and Precedent Study

I researched several projects related to emotional communication, mood tracking, colour systems, and personal data visualisation. These examples helped me think about how emotional information can be organised and displayed visually.

### Mood Tracking Applications

![alt text](../assets/week-06/Screenshot%202026-05-29%20at%205.02.10 PM.png)

I looked at several mood tracking applications that allow users to record their emotions throughout the day. These systems often use simple categories and colours to represent emotional states.

What interested me most was how repeated emotional records can reveal larger patterns over time. Rather than focusing on a single emotion, users can observe trends and changes across days, weeks, or months.

This made me think about emotions as a form of personal data that can be collected and visualised.

---

### Colour and Emotion Systems

![alt text](../assets/week-06/Screenshot%202026-05-29%20at%205.05.02 PM.png)

I also researched colour systems used to represent emotions. Different colours can create different emotional meanings. For example:

- Blue often feels calm or peaceful
- Yellow can feel positive and energetic
- Red can communicate stress or pressure
- Purple can communicate sadness
- Grey can suggest tiredness or low energy

This research helped me think about creating a simple emotional colour system that users can understand quickly.

---

### Time-Based Visualisation

![alt text](../assets/week-06/Screenshot%202026-05-29%20at%205.07.50 PM.png)

I also explored examples of time-based visualisation systems. These projects organise information into days, weeks, or months to help users recognise patterns.

I was particularly interested in how repeated visual structures can make long-term changes easier to understand. This inspired me to think about organising emotions across different days and different times of the day.

This research encouraged me to move beyond emotional imagery and begin exploring emotions as data that can be recorded and compared over time.

---

## Project Planning

The current plan is to create a visual system that organises emotions into categories and displays them across time.

Possible features include:

- Emotion selection buttons
- Colour-based emotional categories
- Time-based emotional records
- Interactive visual layouts
- Mood filtering systems

I also started planning how emotional information could be organised within the project.

The emotional categories may include:

- Happy
- Calm
- Stress
- Sad
- Tired

Each day may be divided into:

- Morning
- Afternoon
- Night

This structure would allow users to compare emotions across different time periods and identify repeated emotional patterns.

![alt text](../assets/week-06/Screenshot%202026-05-29%20at%205.09.43 PM.png)

At this stage, I am still deciding how the interaction should work and what visual style best represents emotional experiences. However, I now have a clearer understanding of the information that needs to be recorded and visualised.

---

## Reflection

This week helped me better understand the relationship between emotions and visual communication. I realised that emotional data does not always need to appear as numbers or statistics. Colours, visual structures, and repeated patterns can also communicate meaningful emotional information.

I also started thinking about emotions as data that can be collected over time. Repeated emotional records may reveal patterns that are difficult to notice in everyday life. For example, users may discover that they often feel stressed before deadlines or calmer during weekends.

Looking at existing examples helped me think more clearly about how emotional experiences might be represented visually and how emotional patterns could be explored through interaction and data visualisation.

For the next stage, I want to begin experimenting with simple layouts, emotional categorisation systems, and interactive prototypes in p5.js. I hope to test whether users can recognise emotional patterns through colour, time, and visual organisation.
