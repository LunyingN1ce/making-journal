## Independent Study

This week I continued developing my emotional data visualisation project. I focused mainly on research, visual references, and planning the overall direction of the project. My idea is to use colours and photographs to categorise emotions and help people better understand emotional experiences.

At this stage, I am still exploring how emotions can be translated into visual language. Instead of using traditional graphs or numerical charts, I want to communicate emotional states through atmosphere, colour relationships, and photographic imagery.

---

## Visual Research and Precedent Study

I researched several projects related to emotional communication, colour systems, and image-based visual organisation. Many visual design projects use colour palettes and grouped imagery to create emotional meaning.

I was especially interested in moodboards and emotional photography collections. Different colours can create very different emotional feelings. For example:

- Blue and green often feel calm or peaceful
- Red and orange may feel energetic or stressful
- Darker colours can communicate sadness or isolation

I also researched projects related to mental wellbeing and emotional awareness. Some projects use visual interaction to help audiences reflect on their emotional state. These examples helped me think more carefully about emotional atmosphere and visual hierarchy.

---

## Project Planning

The current plan is to create a visual system that organises emotions into different categories using colours and photographs.

Possible features include:

- Emotion selection buttons
- Colour-based emotional categories
- Mood image collections
- Interactive visual layouts

Users may choose emotions that match their feelings, and the system will display related colours and photographs. I want the experience to feel personal, reflective, and visually engaging.

At this stage, I am still deciding how the interaction should work and what visual style best represents emotional experiences.

---

## Reflection

This week helped me better understand the relationship between emotions and visual communication. I realised that emotional data does not always need to appear as numbers or statistics. Colours, images, and visual atmosphere can also communicate meaningful emotional information.

I also learned that visual research is important before starting technical development. Looking at existing examples helped me think more clearly about my own project direction and how emotional experiences might be represented visually.

For the next stage, I want to continue exploring visual references and begin experimenting with simple layouts and emotional categorisation systems.
